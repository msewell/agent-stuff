## English variety and spelling

Use one coherent variety of standard English within a page. Preserve the page's established spelling, vocabulary, date form, and punctuation unless a change corrects an error or removes real ambiguity. Do not change an established variety merely to impose a preference.

When no variety can be inferred, write plain international English and avoid unnecessary variant-sensitive vocabulary. Use American spelling only when a spelling choice is unavoidable. Preserve the spelling of direct quotations, titles of works, and proper names.

Use standard dictionary spellings. Do not choose an unusual spelling merely because it looks older, more formal, or more international. Retain diacritics in proper names when they are part of the established spelling. Use the spelling most commonly used in reliable English-language material when the supplied context does not settle the question.

Use the dedicated references for capitalization and abbreviations, text formatting and titles, and pronunciation and non-English text.

## Names and identity

Use a person's, group's, organization's, place's, or work's established name consistently. Preserve the subject's own spelling in a proper name. When sources use more than one name, use the name that the supplied page or reliable local material establishes, and introduce a significant alternative name once where it helps readers recognize the subject.

Do not use honorifics, post-nominal letters, nicknames, slogans, trademarks, or promotional styling unless they are necessary to identify the subject or are part of a direct quotation or formal title. Do not write possessive or plural forms of a name in a way that changes the name itself.

Use the current or historical name that fits the time being described. Avoid anachronism: name a place, office, organization, or political entity as it existed at the relevant time, adding current context only when it helps the reader.
