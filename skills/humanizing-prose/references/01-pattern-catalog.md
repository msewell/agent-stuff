# AI-tell pattern catalog

Use this catalog as an editing checklist. The patterns are suspicion triggers, not automatic deletions. Keep any wording that is true, idiomatic, genre-appropriate, and better than the alternative.

## How to use the catalog

1. Scan for the pattern groups most relevant to the draft.
2. Rewrite the sentence around concrete actors, facts, and stakes.
3. Preserve source facts. Do not invent details, examples, or category instances just to make a sentence feel specific.
4. In non-English text, apply the underlying principle rather than the English surface rule.

## Content patterns

### 1. Inflated significance and legacy claims

**Watch for:** `stands as`, `serves as`, `testament`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `underscores`, `broader trend`, `evolving landscape`, `indelible mark`, `setting the stage`.

**Problem:** The sentence makes an ordinary fact sound historically important without evidence.

**Rewrite toward:** what happened, who did it, when, and why it matters at the correct size.

**Before:** The launch marks a pivotal moment in the evolution of team productivity.

**After:** The launch gives teams shared inboxes, task assignment, and message search in one product.

### 2. Notability and media puffery

**Watch for:** `leading expert`, `widely covered`, `active social media presence`, lists of publications without a specific claim.

**Problem:** The text treats attention as substance.

**Rewrite toward:** the concrete claim, event, or evidence the coverage supports.

### 3. Superficial present-participle analysis

**Watch for:** sentence tails beginning with `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `reflecting`, `showcasing`, `contributing to`, `ensuring`, `fostering`.

**Problem:** The clause adds fake depth after the sentence has already ended.

**Rewrite toward:** a separate concrete sentence or remove the clause.

### 4. Promotional language

**Watch for:** `vibrant`, `rich`, `profound`, `groundbreaking`, `renowned`, `breathtaking`, `must-visit`, `seamless`, `intuitive`, `powerful`, `nestled`, `in the heart of`.

**Problem:** The tone sounds like an advertisement rather than a person making a claim.

**Rewrite toward:** observable qualities, limits, and tradeoffs.

### 5. Vague attribution and weasel sources

**Watch for:** `experts say`, `observers note`, `industry reports`, `some critics argue`, `many believe`, `sources suggest`.

**Problem:** The text borrows authority without naming it.

**Rewrite toward:** named sources already provided, or remove the attribution.

### 6. Formulaic challenge/future sections

**Watch for:** `Despite these challenges`, `future outlook`, `continues to thrive`, `path forward`, `several challenges remain`.

**Problem:** The section summarizes instead of deciding what matters.

**Rewrite toward:** the actual blocker, consequence, owner, or next decision.

## Language and grammar patterns

### 7. Overused AI vocabulary

**Watch for:** `delve`, `tapestry`, `landscape`, `intricate`, `interplay`, `pivotal`, `robust`, `seamless`, `showcase`, `underscore`, `enhance`, `elevate`, `align`, `foster`, `crucial`, `key`.

**Problem:** These words often replace the specific noun or verb the sentence needs.

**Rewrite toward:** plainer words and concrete objects.

### 8. Copula avoidance

**Watch for:** `serves as`, `stands as`, `functions as`, `represents`, `boasts`, `features`, `offers` where `is`, `has`, or a direct verb would work.

**Problem:** The sentence dodges a simple claim.

**Rewrite toward:** `is`, `has`, or a concrete verb.

### 9. Negative parallelism and tailing negation

**Watch for:** `not just X, but Y`, `not only X but also Y`, `it's not about X; it's about Y`, sentence-ending fragments like `no guessing`, `no wasted motion`.

**Problem:** The structure creates drama without adding meaning.

**Rewrite toward:** state the positive claim directly.

### 10. Rule-of-three padding

**Watch for:** forced triples of features, adjectives, benefits, or abstractions.

**Problem:** The rhythm sounds assembled and comprehensive by habit.

**Rewrite toward:** the one or two items that matter, or a natural list if all items are necessary.

### 11. Synonym cycling

**Watch for:** repeated references to the same thing under different names: `tool`, `platform`, `solution`, `system`, `offering`.

**Problem:** Variation hides continuity and makes prose sound generated.

**Rewrite toward:** use the same term consistently unless the distinction matters.

### 12. False ranges

**Watch for:** `from X to Y` where X and Y are not endpoints on a real scale.

**Problem:** The phrase pretends to organize breadth.

**Rewrite toward:** name the actual topics or examples.

### 13. Passive voice and subjectless fragments

**Watch for:** `it was decided`, `has been completed`, `is handled automatically`, `No setup required`.

**Problem:** The actor disappears, or the sentence becomes product-copy shorthand.

**Rewrite toward:** a clear actor when the actor matters. Keep passive voice when it protects accuracy or reads better.

## Style patterns

### 14. Dash overuse

**Watch for:** repeated em dashes, especially in punchy marketing or essay prose.

**Problem:** Dashes create artificial drama and identical cadence.

**Rewrite toward:** commas, periods, parentheses, or sentence restructuring. Keep a dash if it is the best punctuation for the language and voice.

### 15. Mechanical boldface

**Watch for:** bolded keywords in every bullet or paragraph.

**Problem:** The formatting tells the reader what to value instead of earning emphasis.

**Rewrite toward:** plain text, shorter sentences, or fewer sections.

### 16. Inline-header bullet lists

**Watch for:** bullets that begin with bold labels plus colons: `**Speed:**`, `**Security:**`, `**Flexibility:**`.

**Problem:** The list often turns prose into a slide deck.

**Rewrite toward:** paragraphs or bullets whose structure reflects the actual content. Keep labels for scan-heavy operational docs.

### 17. Generic title-case headings

**Watch for:** default title case in informal docs, emails, or essays.

**Problem:** It can make headings feel templated.

**Rewrite toward:** sentence case unless the style guide, language, or publication expects title case.

### 18. Decorative emojis

**Watch for:** emoji markers in headings, bullets, launch copy, or checklists.

**Problem:** They often simulate energy instead of adding information.

**Rewrite toward:** words. Keep emojis only when they fit the medium and the user's voice.

### 19. Punctuation normalization mistakes

**Watch for:** changing quotation marks, apostrophes, dashes, decimal separators, or spacing conventions without a reason.

**Problem:** Mechanical normalization can damage typography or language conventions.

**Rewrite toward:** preserve the user's or locale's convention unless the target style guide says otherwise.

## Communication patterns

### 20. Chatbot residue

**Watch for:** `Certainly`, `Of course`, `Great question`, `I hope this helps`, `Let me know if`, `Here is an overview`.

**Problem:** Assistant chatter leaks into the user's prose.

**Rewrite toward:** start with the content itself.

### 21. Knowledge-cutoff hedging

**Watch for:** `as of my last update`, `based on available information`, `specific details are limited`, `it appears that`.

**Problem:** The text carries model limitations into the draft.

**Rewrite toward:** a sourced claim, a narrower claim, or an explicit placeholder for missing facts.

### 22. Sycophantic tone

**Watch for:** excessive praise of the reader or prompt: `excellent point`, `you're absolutely right`, `great question`.

**Problem:** The prose flatters instead of thinking.

**Rewrite toward:** acknowledge the useful point without performance.

## Filler and hedging patterns

### 23. Filler phrases

**Watch for:** `in order to`, `due to the fact that`, `at this point in time`, `it is important to note`, `has the ability to`, `in the event that`.

**Rewrite toward:** `to`, `because`, `now`, direct statement, `can`, `if`.

### 24. Excessive hedging

**Watch for:** stacked qualifiers: `could potentially`, `might possibly`, `it may be argued that`.

**Problem:** The sentence refuses to own its claim.

**Rewrite toward:** the smallest accurate claim.

### 25. Generic positive conclusions

**Watch for:** `the future looks bright`, `exciting times ahead`, `major step forward`, `journey toward excellence`.

**Problem:** The ending stops saying anything.

**Rewrite toward:** the actual next step, consequence, uncertainty, or decision.

### 26. Compound-modifier overpatterning

**Watch for:** unusually consistent hyphenation or compound phrases: `cross-functional`, `data-driven`, `client-facing`, `real-time`, `end-to-end`.

**Problem:** AI prose often overuses these compounds.

**Rewrite toward:** fewer compounds or more direct nouns. Preserve correct hyphenation when the compound modifies a noun or the style guide requires it.

### 27. Persuasive authority tropes

**Watch for:** `the real question is`, `at its core`, `fundamentally`, `what really matters`, `the heart of the matter`, `the deeper issue`.

**Problem:** The phrase pretends to cut through complexity.

**Rewrite toward:** state the question or issue directly.

### 28. Signposting and announcements

**Watch for:** `let's dive in`, `let's explore`, `let's break this down`, `here's what you need to know`, `without further ado`.

**Problem:** The writer announces instead of writing.

**Rewrite toward:** begin with the claim, fact, or instruction.

### 29. Fragmented warm-up headings

**Watch for:** a heading followed by a one-line paragraph that restates it before the content begins.

**Problem:** The section starts twice.

**Rewrite toward:** delete the warm-up or fold it into the first substantive sentence.
