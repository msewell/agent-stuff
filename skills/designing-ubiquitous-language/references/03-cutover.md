# Shared-model cutover

1. **Bound the slice.** Inventory actual readers, writers, grants, and background jobs. Record who owns the fact before and after cutover; stop new shared-model dependencies. Put a thin translation around the legacy source, not another universal model.
2. **Backfill and shadow.** Tie a consistent snapshot to its change-log position; make replay idempotent. Reconcile on stable keys at business grain and a known cutoff. Keep legacy flags without qualifying evidence unknown or in review, with provenance; don’t promote them to confirmed facts. Shadow the decision without executing side effects such as postings, stock movements, or notifications. Turn approved differences into fixtures instead of demanding blind parity.
3. **Transfer authority by cohort.** Before changing routing or grants, require evidence of reconciliation, acceptable lag, and rehearsed recovery. At handover, enforce and verify the fence on the former writer before enabling the new writer. Verify the first authoritative write; old consumers can read a faithful projection from the new authority if needed.
4. **Recover according to where failure occurred.** Before transfer, rollback may be a route switch. After authoritative writes, don’t switch back without a proven way to preserve or explicitly reverse those writes. If neither works safely, retain authority and repair forward.
5. **Retire on evidence.** Observe relevant business cycles and scheduled jobs; verify no calls or grants remain and any audit or replay path is accounted for. Remove the superseded route, permissions, and dependency. Keep required history under an explicit owner.
