# Making Software Architecture Decisions

An architecture choice is a context-dependent bet. Make the uncertainty, sacrifices, decision rights, and cheapest decision-changing evidence visible. Do not turn this into an ADR-format exercise.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the choice, not the proposed technology.** State the business outcome, affected journey, scope, current design, timing, and the next commitment that would close an option. Distinguish binding requirements from preferences and forecasts. If a missing fact could reverse the recommendation, ask a targeted question; otherwise proceed with explicit assumptions and a conditional conclusion. Do not pretend an unconfirmed customer promise, compliance interpretation, or availability target is settled.
2. **Scale the effort.** Check blast radius, cross-team dependencies, uncertainty, and the *actual* reversal cost—including persistent data, external contracts, operational work, and customers—not merely `git revert`. Delegate cheap, team-local choices to the team; give irreversible or regulated choices specialist review before commitment. Defer only if waiting can produce decision-changing information without losing an option. A discount or vendor deadline is an opportunity cost, not evidence that a risky design is safe; explicitly compare losing the offer with committing under uncertainty. Never assume that a contract can be cancelled, conditioned, or deferred without confirming its terms and the procurement owner's authority.
3. **Discover drivers and boundaries.** Translate requests such as “use microservices” or “100% uptime” into the flows and outcomes they serve. In a domain-boundary dispute, model real events and existing ownership before drawing services. For a developer platform, observe a representative developer journey and test a minimum viable self-service path before mandating adoption. Consult [method routing and evidence](references/01-method-routing.md) when choosing a workshop or analytic method.
4. **Specify success as scenarios.** For the most important qualities, describe the stimulus, environment, expected response, and measurable response criterion. Cover a failure or change scenario, not just normal operation. Classify confirmed security, privacy, contractual, and legal constraints as *gates*; do not bury them in weighted scoring. If applicability is unknown, seek the actual control owner's interpretation before using protected data; do not assume that data location alone proves compliance, that another option passes the gate, or that an assumed reviewer has veto authority. Confirm targets with their owners instead of inventing numbers.
5. **Compare credible options.** Include the current design with a targeted improvement or doing less where feasible; avoid a false binary between buzzwords. Compare candidates against the **same** scenarios. Name each option’s benefits, sacrificed qualities, migration steps, operating burden, and who bears the cost. Use a small annotated trade-off matrix only as a discussion aid; test whether changing subjective weights changes the apparent winner.
6. **Buy evidence where it can change the choice.** Separate observed facts, estimates, and guesses. Pick the riskiest falsifiable premise; propose a representative, time-boxed test with a failure criterion and owner (e.g., restore drill, representative load, security threat model, migration rehearsal, or one cross-team change). For stateful/financial flows, check the end-to-end outcome through duplicates, partial success, replay, and recovery; naming a pattern such as idempotency does not prove it prevents double charges or lost orders. For unpredictable futures, optionally stress a preliminary design with plausible counterfactual changes, revise it, and test against a held-out set. Treat imagined stressors as a challenge exercise, never proof of future demand. Do not pilot with live customers or protected data before relevant safety and legal gates are cleared.
7. **Set decision rights and a finite endpoint.** Seek advice from people meaningfully affected and people with expertise. Ask who is accountable for the decision and who actually has approval or blocking authority; never infer either from a job title or from who requested the change. Seek dissent early, collect feedback asynchronously first, and time-box escalation. Choose, reject, narrow an experiment, or state exactly which unresolved gate prevents commitment; never manufacture consensus.
8. **Plan learning after commitment.** Specify a limited rollout, data/API compatibility and *real* rollback or forward-repair conditions. A correctness test is not a rollback rehearsal; validate these separately. Translate the key prediction into a measurable check with owner and response. Name a trigger for reconsideration if constraints, operational burden, or observed outcomes change. A feature flag does not reverse destructive data changes.

## Default response

Keep the response proportional to the decision. For “be brief,” aim for roughly 150 words: lead with the decision or blocker, give only the most consequential alternative and decisive next evidence, and omit a table and unnecessary sections. Do not mention the skill or its instructions unless asked. For a fuller analysis, give:

- **Recommendation or explicit pending decision:** what to choose *under stated assumptions*, or what binding information is missing; name the decider if known.
- **Why it fits:** business driver and top quality scenarios; distinguish known constraints from assumptions.
- **Alternatives and sacrifices:** include the sensible current-state option; state the strongest reason the preferred choice could be wrong.
- **Decisive next evidence:** the smallest useful experiment, failure criterion, owner, and deadline if available; do not invent a timeline.
- **Commitment and revisit:** rollout/rollback reality, remaining risk owner, observable signal, and reassessment trigger.

Before sending, remove unsupported quantitative claims as well as proposed numeric thresholds, sample sizes, staffing cutoffs, adoption percentages, or deadlines not supplied by the user or verified evidence; even phrases such as “this week,” “this morning,” or “one sprint” invent a schedule when the user has not supplied one. When a binding requirement is unknown, recommend pausing the affected commitment and testing alternatives, not prescribing a preferred implementation or asserting that an unverified fallback is compliant. Do not claim a proposed migration cannot meet its deadline without evidence. If the user specifically asks for illustrative numbers, mark them as non-binding hypotheses for local agreement, never decision gates. Audit every reversibility claim: a pilot, modular system, or feature flag may still create persistent data, external interfaces, or organizational lock-in; call rollback **unproven** until rehearsed. Do not invent a contract clause, risk owner, or legal mandate. Do not supply an ADR template, arbitrary score, universal architecture pattern, or contractual promise. If asked for an ADR format separately, handle that as a separate documentation task; this workflow determines *how to decide*.

## Example

**Input:** “Six engineers want microservices to move faster; traffic and boundaries are unknown.”

**Approach:** Probe what “faster” means; compare a modular existing/new application with services against the same change and recovery scenarios; quantify the *team's* operational capacity; test one representative cross-boundary change. Recommend conditionally and name what evidence would justify extraction. Do not assert a magical team size, traffic threshold, or easy extraction/rollback.

**Unknown tenant-isolation requirement:** “Pause the fleet-wide migration. First ask the contract and restore owners what isolation is promised. Compare shared, selective, and full isolation against that promise; rehearse access controls, restore, migration, and reversal under safe conditions. None is approved until it meets the confirmed gate. Name the decider and any required sign-off according to your actual governance.” Do not prescribe encryption keys or a tenant-specific pilot without evidence that they address the requirement.

**Unknown data-residency requirement:** “Do not send protected data through either vendor yet. Ask the authorized control owner what the clause permits and independently check *each* candidate against it. The higher-scoring vendor is not automatically disqualified, and the lower-scoring one is not automatically compliant. Confirm who can approve or block before selecting among viable choices.”

**Expiring queue discount, untested checkout recovery:** “Identify which guarantees are required before production and whether either queue can meet them. Compare the discount with the risk of locking in a design that loses orders or double-charges. If the deadline precludes meaningful testing, decide explicitly whether to forgo the discount or, **only if the actual contract permits a limited or terminable commitment**, accept it with no production traffic until end-to-end recovery and rollback have been separately verified. Do not claim an idempotency key solves the whole workflow, a quick sandbox test proves production safety, or that the vendor will grant an exit condition.”
